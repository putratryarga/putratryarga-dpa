# T16 — Community Management & Advocacy: 12-Chapter Source-Fit Review

**Review date:** 5 November 2026  
**Disposition:** First-pass desk review complete; all 12 chapters PARTIAL. Formal objective fit, Indonesia access, legal applicability and SME approval remain OPEN.

## Executive finding

T16 previously relied mainly on Meta Blueprint community courses, NSW Government community-management guidance, YouTube moderation settings and Kumparan Cyber Media Guidelines. The new source pass adds Indonesia's UU PDP 27/2022, UU ITE 1/2024, PP 71/2019, Meta Safety Center controls/policies, TikTok comment filters and Community Guidelines, and YouTube's channel/live-chat moderation tools.

These sources describe different layers of the operating environment; they must not be conflated:

1. **Indonesian law:** regulates personal-data processing, electronic systems and certain forms of unlawful electronic content. A community manager should route legal interpretation and reportable incidents to qualified legal/privacy personnel.
2. **Platform policies and controls:** show what Meta, TikTok and YouTube allow and how their moderation tools work. Product/account availability and policy text can change.
3. **Journalistic standards:** Dewan Pers Cyber Media Guidelines apply to cyber media conducting journalistic activity; they are not automatically a moderation rulebook for an ordinary brand social account.
4. **Brand-community SOP:** defines member experience, response SLA, moderation thresholds, escalation, customer-care handoff, evidence handling and business ownership. Existing sources do not provide a complete private-brand SOP for Indonesia.

All 12 chapters remain PARTIAL because the current resources do not validate end-to-end private-sector community operations, social-listening methodology, complaint handling, creator/UGC permission, or an Indonesia-specific crisis playbook.

## Indonesian legal and regulatory references

### 1. UU No. 27 Tahun 2022 — Pelindungan Data Pribadi

The official JDIH record identifies UU PDP No. 27/2022 and its status. The law covers personal-data subject rights and processing principles. Its text includes requirements for a valid processing basis, limited/specific/lawful/transparent collection, purpose limitation, accuracy, protection, accountability and notification of certain personal-data-protection failures. It is directly relevant when community teams collect, store, export or share identifiable customer/member information from comments, DMs, groups, forms or CRM.

Source: [JDIH DPR — UU 27/2022 Pelindungan Data Pribadi](https://jdih.dpr.go.id/setjen/detail-dokumen/tipe/uu/id/1814).

### 2. UU No. 1 Tahun 2024 — Perubahan Kedua atas UU ITE

The JDIH record identifies UU 1/2024 as the second amendment to the ITE Law and records it as in force. The text includes government authority to order access termination and/or moderation of certain unlawful or harmful electronic information. It provides a legal context for platform/content moderation; it is not a detailed operational standard for a brand moderator to adjudicate every complaint.

Source: [JDIH Komdigi — UU 1/2024](https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/view/id/884/t/undangundang%20nomor%201%20tahun%202024).

### 3. PP No. 71 Tahun 2019 — Penyelenggaraan Sistem dan Transaksi Elektronik

The regulation sets governance/security responsibilities for Electronic System Providers (PSE), including system reliability, accountability, risk management and procedures. It is relevant to the broader platform/provider ecosystem. Do not assume every brand-community manager or ordinary brand social account is itself a regulated PSE; legal classification and obligations depend on the actual service, entity and facts.

Source: [JDIH Komdigi — PP 71/2019](https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/view/id/695/t/peraturan%2Bpemerintah%2Bnomor%2B71%2Btahun%2B2019).

## Platform-native moderation and safety evidence

### Meta

Meta Safety Center describes account restriction, comment controls, hiding offensive words, profanity filters and reporting abuse. Its Community Standards define platform content rules across Facebook, Instagram, Threads and Messenger. These are platform rules and tools, not Indonesian legal standards or a substitute for an organization's human review, escalation and appeal process.

Sources: [Meta — Stay safe and manage interactions](https://www.meta.com/safety/topics/safety-basics/tools/stay-safe/) and [Meta — Safety Policies / Community Standards](https://www.meta.com/safety/topics/safety-basics/policies/).

### TikTok

TikTok's comment controls allow creators to filter spam, offensive comments, keyword matches and other content for review. The official Community Guidelines apply across TikTok content and account activity; the page changes over time, so the reviewer should record the active version/effective date when used in a course or operating playbook. Automated filters are triage aids, not perfect classifiers: keep human review, appeals and escalation for context-sensitive cases.

Sources: [TikTok — Manage comments and filters](https://support.tiktok.com/en/account-and-privacy/account-privacy-settings/manage-comments) and [TikTok Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/).

### YouTube

YouTube Studio provides content controls for comments, live chat and Community posts, blocked words and links, plus holding content for review. Live-chat tools allow moderators, basic/strict hold settings and slow mode. The platform's settings and labels may change; account permissions and the difference between standard and managing moderators must be tested on the actual channel.

Sources: [YouTube — Manage channel moderation settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16622701?hl=en) and [YouTube — Moderate live chat](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9826490?hl=en).

## Journalism guidance and community-management practice

### Dewan Pers — Pedoman Pemberitaan Media Siber

The guideline applies to cyber media that conduct journalistic activity and are subject to press-law and journalistic-ethics obligations. It can inform transparency, corrections and editorial accountability where a brand works with a publisher or runs a journalistic media product. It should not be presented as binding rules for all ordinary brand-community comments, DMs or private groups.

Source: [Dewan Pers — Pedoman Pemberitaan Media Siber](https://storage.dewanpers.or.id/websiteprod/content/20250616033436-Buku%20Saku%20Cetakan%20ke%2022%20Agustus%202024.pdf).

### NSW Government — Community Management for Social Media

The NSW guide provides practical examples of community guidelines, monitoring, active/passive/hands-off moderation, responding to negative feedback, escalation and crisis response. It is government-context guidance from New South Wales, not Indonesian law or a substitute for brand policy, customer-care SLAs or local privacy/legal review.

Source: [NSW Government — Community Management for Social Media](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/communications/social-media-guidelines/community-management).

## Operational guardrails

1. Do not copy or publish personal data from comments/DMs into public response documents, portfolio files or third-party spreadsheets without a defined purpose and approved handling basis.
2. Classify moderation severity and preserve only the evidence needed under the approved retention and escalation process. Platform filters should not replace human review for context-dependent complaints or safety concerns.
3. Keep separate routes for platform-policy reports, customer service, privacy requests, legal matters and credible safety/crisis signals.
4. Do not treat all criticism as abuse or misinformation. Preserve legitimate complaints and good-faith disagreement; apply transparent rules consistently.
5. Record platform, account type, tool availability, moderator permission and last-checked date. A setting shown in documentation may not be available to every account.
6. Require Indonesian legal/privacy review before translating UU PDP, UU ITE or PP 71 into a brand-specific SOP. This review is a curriculum desk assessment, not legal advice.
