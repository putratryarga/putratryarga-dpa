**Version:** 1.0 · **Date:** 4 November 2026  
**Taxonomy:** 16 core tracks + 2 advanced electives  
**Status:** Advanced curriculum specification; evidence validation partial.

## 1. Scope and method

This audit preserves the existing 16-core + 2-elective architecture. The five current holding groups are WPP Media, Publicis Media, Omnicom Media, Dentsu and Havas Media Network. Legacy IPG/Mediabrands evidence may be retained as historical/network evidence where identity is clear; it is not counted as a sixth independent holding group after Omnicom's acquisition.

The 10 evidence groups are the five agency groups crossed with the two electives: WPP × CRO, Publicis × CRO, Omnicom × CRO, Dentsu × CRO, Havas × CRO, and the same five for Traditional Media.

Evidence is not forced to be symmetrical. Missing direct evidence is a finding, not permission to infer an equivalent role.

### Evidence classes

| Code | Class | Use |
|---|---|---|
| A | Direct role evidence | Strongest basis for role-specific competencies |
| B | Adjacent role evidence | Supports only the overlapping responsibilities explicitly present |
| C | Agency capability/service evidence | Confirms a capability exists, not detailed job-level requirements |
| D | Third-party, historical or syndicated listing | Corroboration; verify against official employer evidence |
| E | Industry standard/vendor documentation | Supports technical or methodological content, not role existence |

Confidence: **High** = direct official role evidence; **Medium** = adjacent, integrated, third-party or market-specific; **Low/Open** = indirect or insufficient evidence. Geography, date, title and scope must remain attached to each record.

## 5. Chapter-by-chapter and subchapter audit

A chapter is not complete merely because its title matches a competency. Every subchapter needs: competency ID; observable learning objective; job-task rationale or labelled adjacent evidence; advanced practice artifact; assessment criteria; source/market limitations. The following 28-chapter specification expands the current proposed 14 + 14 structures. It is a content specification to implement, not proof the website already contains it.

### 5A. Elective 17 — CRO / Growth / Experience Optimization

#### Chapter 1. Growth Economics & Conversion Strategy
**Mapping:** CRO-01, CRO-12. **Verdict:** Keep; deepen unit economics and commercial decision-making.
1. Business model, value chain and conversion event economics: revenue model, margin structure, lead-to-sale chain and decision owner.
2. Funnel economics and KPI tree: acquisition cost, conversion rate, AOV, LTV, payback, contribution margin and guardrails.
3. Opportunity sizing and sensitivity: baseline, addressable volume, expected lift ranges, downside/base/upside and uncertainty.
4. Strategy translation: business objective → customer behaviour → conversion objective → measurement → investment decision.
**Build:** business/conversion model with KPI tree and sensitivity analysis. **Pass:** every metric has formula, denominator, period, source and decision use; simulated assumptions are labelled. **Gap:** explain cohort, gross-margin, attribution and payback assumptions.

#### Chapter 2. Customer Research & Insight
**Mapping:** CRO-02, CRO-05. **Verdict:** Keep; add rigorous research design and synthesis.
1. Research framing: problem statement, research question, decision and evidence gap.
2. Qualitative methods: interviews, usability tests, observation, task success and moderation bias.
3. Quantitative methods: surveys, behavioural segmentation, funnel data and sampling limitations.
4. Synthesis: triangulation, affinity themes, severity, confidence, customer jobs and insight-to-hypothesis translation.
**Build:** research protocol, screener, interview/usability guide and insight repository. **Pass:** distinguish observed behaviour, stated preference, interpretation and hypothesis. **Gap:** include recruitment bias, consent and privacy.

#### Chapter 3. Digital Analytics Architecture
**Mapping:** CRO-03, CRO-10. **Verdict:** Keep; strengthen implementation governance and privacy.
1. Measurement planning: business questions, event taxonomy, parameters, naming and KPI ownership.
2. Instrumentation architecture: browser/app events, data layer, tag management, server-side considerations and identity boundaries.
3. Data quality: event validation, deduplication, consent, missingness, bot/internal traffic, timezone and currency.
4. Governance: data contracts, change control, access, retention, privacy, QA and incident handling.
**Build:** event specification, data-layer map, tagging plan and QA log. **Pass:** each event maps to a decision; unnecessary personal data is excluded. **Gap:** analytics architecture is not synonymous with GA4 setup.

#### Chapter 4. Behavioural & Funnel Analytics
**Mapping:** CRO-03, CRO-04. **Verdict:** Keep; deepen diagnostic logic and segmentation.
1. Funnel definition: entry, progression, conversion, drop-off and event semantics.
2. Segmentation: device, channel, source, audience, new/returning, product, cohort and eligibility.
3. Behavioural diagnostics: pathing, search behaviour, rage/dead clicks, scroll and session replay limitations.
4. Opportunity sizing: leakage analysis, denominator checks, confidence ranges and prioritization inputs.
**Build:** segmented funnel diagnosis and quantified opportunity backlog. **Pass:** reconcile event counts with business systems; heatmaps are not causal proof. **Gap:** add cohort and data-quality checks before recommending UX changes.

#### Chapter 5. UX & Digital Experience Optimization
**Mapping:** CRO-02, CRO-05. **Verdict:** Keep; add accessibility, task design and product constraints.
1. Experience heuristics and information architecture: navigation, hierarchy, findability and comprehension.
2. Landing pages and message match: ad/query promise, offer, proof, relevance and CTA.
3. Forms, checkout and transaction flow: field friction, validation, payment, errors, trust and abandonment.
4. Mobile, accessibility and performance: responsive behaviour, keyboard/screen-reader considerations, load performance and inclusive design.
**Build:** UX/CX audit, task-based usability report and prioritized prototype. **Pass:** recommendations tie to observed user task failures and business constraints. **Gap:** aesthetic preference is not conversion evidence.

#### Chapter 6. Hypothesis & Opportunity Prioritization
**Mapping:** CRO-04, CRO-06, CRO-12. **Verdict:** Keep; require falsifiable hypotheses and economic prioritization.
1. Observation-to-insight chain: evidence, interpretation, root-cause candidate and uncertainty.
2. Hypothesis construction: audience, change, mechanism, expected effect, primary metric and guardrail.
3. Opportunity sizing: reach, baseline, potential impact, confidence interval and implementation cost.
4. Portfolio prioritization: ICE/PIE as aids, dependencies, risk, strategic fit and sequencing.
**Build:** evidence-backed experiment backlog. **Pass:** reject hypotheses without a measurable mechanism or with gameable success metrics. **Gap:** scores are aids, not objective truth.

#### Chapter 7. Experimentation Design
**Mapping:** CRO-06, CRO-07. **Verdict:** Keep; make pre-registration and test validity explicit.
1. Experiment types: A/B, multivariate, split URL, geo/holdout and quasi-experimental alternatives.
2. Design mechanics: randomization unit, eligibility, assignment, exposure, contamination and interference.
3. Metric design: primary outcome, diagnostics, guardrails, minimum practical effect and business relevance.
4. Protocol: sample-size approach, duration assumptions, stopping rule, rollout, rollback and documentation.
**Build:** pre-registered experiment brief and design-review checklist. **Pass:** randomization and analysis units align; avoid peeking-driven false positives. **Gap:** explain infeasible or unethical tests and acceptable alternatives.

#### Chapter 8. Advanced Statistics for Experimentation
**Mapping:** CRO-07, CRO-08. **Verdict:** Essential advanced chapter; separate significance from practical value.
1. Probability and inference: distributions, sampling, standard error, confidence intervals and uncertainty.
2. Power and sample size: baseline rate, minimum detectable effect, variance, allocation and traffic constraints.
3. Validity threats: multiple comparisons, novelty effects, sample-ratio mismatch, instrumentation errors and peeking.
4. Result interpretation: effect size, uncertainty, practical significance, segment heterogeneity and replication.
**Build:** sample-size worksheet and statistical analysis report. **Pass:** conclusions include effect size and uncertainty, not only a p-value. **Gap:** no universal 95% rule, fixed duration or sample count without design context.

#### Chapter 9. Experimentation Technology & Technical QA
**Mapping:** CRO-03, CRO-07, CRO-10. **Verdict:** Keep; split strategist and technical implementation depth.
1. Platform architecture: experimentation tools, feature flags, SDKs, server/client-side testing and assignment persistence.
2. Implementation: variants, targeting, exposure event, tracking integration and release workflow.
3. Technical QA: rendering, flicker, cross-browser/device, performance, accessibility and event validation.
4. Operational integrity: sample-ratio mismatch, experiment collision, rollback, incident log and version control.
**Build:** implementation specification, QA matrix and test evidence log. **Pass:** assignment and exposure are verified end-to-end; do not claim live tool access when only prototype/sandbox was used. **Gap:** Google Optimize is discontinued; do not list as active.

#### Chapter 10. Causal Measurement & Incrementality
**Mapping:** CRO-08, CRO-09, CRO-12. **Verdict:** Keep as a distinct chapter.
1. Causal questions and estimands: outcome, population, treatment and counterfactual.
2. Randomized holdouts and conversion lift: assignment, exposure, outcome matching and limitations.
3. Geo/matched-market tests: market selection, pre-period fit, contamination and difference-in-differences caveats.
4. Media-to-site connection: conversion lift, incrementality, channel interaction and investment decisions.
**Build:** incrementality design memo with assumptions and threats. **Pass:** state counterfactual, unit, time window, treatment and confounders. **Gap:** platform lift, site A/B, geo tests and MMM answer different questions.

#### Chapter 11. Personalization & Journey Orchestration
**Mapping:** CRO-03, CRO-05, CRO-11. **Verdict:** Keep; add decision logic, consent and failure modes.
1. Segmentation and eligibility: behavioural, contextual, lifecycle and declared preference signals.
2. Decisioning: rules, prioritization, suppression, frequency and conflict resolution.
3. Experience orchestration: web/app/email/CRM handoffs, journey state and channel consistency.
4. Measurement and governance: incremental value, holdouts, privacy, bias, explainability and fallback.
**Build:** personalization decision matrix and measurement plan. **Pass:** do not assume personalization improves outcomes; compare to a valid baseline/control and document consent. **Gap:** avoid duplicating CRM automation; CRO owns experience decisioning and conversion impact.

#### Chapter 12. Experimentation Programme Management
**Mapping:** CRO-06, CRO-07, CRO-10, CRO-12. **Verdict:** Keep; elevate governance and repeatability.
1. Operating model: roles, intake, triage, research, design, engineering, analytics and client approval.
2. Portfolio governance: prioritization cadence, capacity, risk, dependency and experiment calendar.
3. Knowledge management: hypothesis repository, result archive, decision log, reproducibility and negative learning.
4. Quality and capability: peer review, training, documentation, ethics and programme health.
**Build:** CRO operating model, RACI, repository schema and quarterly roadmap. **Pass:** each experiment has owner, approver, QA, decision record and learning disposition. **Gap:** experiment count is not a success metric without quality and impact.

#### Chapter 13. Commercial Impact & Executive Advisory
**Mapping:** CRO-01, CRO-09, CRO-12. **Verdict:** Keep; require defensible financial translation.
1. Revenue and contribution impact: incremental conversions, revenue/visitor, margin, refunds and downstream quality.
2. Business cases: cost-to-build, opportunity range, payback, risk and alternative investment.
3. Executive communication: decision memo, narrative, caveats, trade-offs and recommendation.
4. Client advisory: scope, stakeholder alignment, change management, capability roadmap and account growth.
**Build:** executive business case and client recommendation deck. **Pass:** separate observed results from forecast, modelled impact and simulated values. **Gap:** do not annualize short-term lift without seasonality, capacity and persistence caveats.

#### Chapter 14. Senior CRO Capstone
**Mapping:** CRO-01–CRO-12. **Verdict:** Keep; assess integrated senior ownership, not certificates.
1. Diagnose a defined digital business problem and establish evidence quality.
2. Build customer research, analytics architecture and funnel diagnosis.
3. Develop a prioritized experiment portfolio with valid designs and technical QA.
4. Evaluate causal/commercial impact, governance and executive recommendation.
**Build:** end-to-end CRO programme portfolio, decision log, measurement appendix and executive defense. **Pass:** rubric scores research, data integrity, validity, technical feasibility, commercial reasoning, governance and communication. **Gap:** data must be permitted real data or explicitly synthetic; never invent client outcomes.

## 8. Chapter quality gate and scoring rubric

Every chapter/subchapter must pass all six gates:

| Gate | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Role relevance | Explicit competency ID and real job responsibility; adjacent evidence labelled. |
| Depth | Method, decision logic, failure modes, trade-offs and advanced application, not definitions only. |
| Source quality | Exact source, issuer/employer, date, geography, evidence class and limitations. |
| Practice | Observable artifact using permitted real data or clearly labelled synthetic data. |
| Assessment | Answer key/rubric with observable criteria, not generic MCQ alone. |
| Boundary | No unexplained duplication of another role's core skill; interface and depth are explicit. |

Suggested four-level rubric:
- **0 — Not demonstrated:** incorrect, missing or unsupported.
- **1 — Foundational:** explains concepts and completes guided task with substantial support.
- **2 — Independent practitioner:** completes realistic task, validates work and explains decisions/limitations.
- **3 — Advanced / lead-ready:** handles ambiguity, optimizes trade-offs, governs quality, advises stakeholders and connects work to business outcomes.

A chapter is advanced when learners make defensible decisions under constraints, validate evidence, quantify trade-offs, manage risk and communicate consequences.

## 9. Resource, credential and Indonesia-market controls

- Course-completion credential, platform learning achievement, skilling certificate, professional/proctored certification and award are separate categories.
- Vendor tutorial is a learning source, not proof the vendor platform is used by every agency.
- Free badge is not automatically professional certification; paid or experience-gated exams must not be mandatory for beginners.
- Verify URL, availability, price, language, eligibility, exam format, expiry/renewal and regional access before a resource is required.
- International measurement examples must be market-labelled. BARC and BARB are not substitutes for Indonesia's local audience measurement ecosystem. MRC is a standards/audit reference, not a local ratings provider.
- Platform confidence thresholds, objectives and benchmarks are not universal rules without methodology.
