## 2. Elective 17 — CRO / Growth / Experience Optimization

### Purpose and scope

Develop the ability to diagnose and improve conversion performance across websites, apps, and digital customer journeys through customer research, analytics, UX, experimentation, causal measurement, personalization, and programme leadership.

**Boundary:** CRO focuses on improving conversion and experience within a defined digital journey. Growth Marketing is broader and may include acquisition, activation, retention, referral, and monetization. The terms are not interchangeable.

### Sub-specialization pathways

1. **CRO Strategy & Experimentation:** research synthesis, opportunity sizing, hypotheses, test portfolio, statistics, causal measurement, and programme leadership.
2. **UX & Conversion Experience Optimization:** usability, information architecture, interaction design, accessibility, journey friction, and conversion-focused evaluation.
3. **CRO Technical Implementation:** analytics instrumentation, event QA, experimentation platform implementation, exposure integrity, and technical measurement.

These are sub-specializations within the elective, not additional roadmap tracks.

## 3. Elective 18 — Traditional Media Planning & Buying

### Purpose and scope

Develop the ability to plan, evaluate, negotiate, buy, deliver, and govern offline media investment across television, radio/audio, print, out-of-home (OOH/DOOH), cinema, sponsorship, and integrated media plans.

**Boundary:** The elective focuses on traditional/offline media investment and buying disciplines. Digital media buying, programmatic trading, and paid social/search remain in their respective core tracks. Integrated planning can connect channels without collapsing distinct buying mechanics or measurement standards.

## 4. Seniority and role expectations

Seniority must be assessed by scope and accountability, not title alone. Group Head, Associate Director, Director, Senior Manager, and AVP titles vary across agencies and markets.

### CRO

- **Senior Specialist:** independently diagnoses conversion problems, designs valid experiments, interprets data, coordinates implementation, and communicates limitations.
- **Manager / Associate Director:** owns an experimentation roadmap, prioritizes a portfolio, manages cross-functional delivery, assures measurement quality, develops team capability, and links work to commercial outcomes.
- **Head / Director:** sets the optimization operating model, governance, investment priorities, senior client agenda, team strategy, and business-impact accountability.

### Traditional Media

- **Senior Specialist:** independently develops channel plans, evaluates audience evidence, negotiates within authority, manages buying execution, and reconciles delivery.
- **Manager / Group Head:** owns integrated offline planning or buying workstreams, partner relationships, budget stewardship, delivery quality, team development, and client recommendations.
- **Head / Director / AVP:** sets investment strategy, commercial governance, senior partner relationships, resource priorities, client growth agenda, and business-unit accountability.

## 5. Evidence and validation rules

The architecture draws on role evidence and industry practice across WPP Media, Publicis Media, Omnicom Media, Dentsu/iProspect, and Havas Media Network. Available role evidence is not perfectly comparable:

- Some CRO evidence comes from adjacent analytics, advanced measurement, or experimentation leadership roles rather than CRO-only titles. It supports relevant competencies but does not prove every agency has an identically named CRO function.
- Some Traditional Media evidence comes from integrated planning/investment roles rather than offline-only roles. It supports senior investment and governance competencies but does not prove all responsibilities are traditional-only.
- Job titles and seniority differ by geography and agency structure. Compare responsibilities, scope, and accountability rather than title labels.
- Audience measurement, inventory practices, and regulation differ by market. Do not transfer country-specific standards (for example, India or UK measurement systems) to Indonesia without local validation.
- Vendor guidance is useful for technical practice but is not automatically a universal statistical or industry standard.

### Required chapter-level evidence register

For each chapter, record: competency claim; employer and role title; geography and publication date; source URL; evidence class (direct role, adjacent role, industry standard, vendor documentation, or learning resource); confidence and limitations; market applicability; last-checked date; and reviewer decision.

## 6. Learning resources and credentials

Resources and credentials are **not frozen** in this architecture. Before inclusion as required learning, verify current working URL, price, free/paid status, language, access, prerequisites, exam format, expiry/renewal, and regional eligibility.

### CRO candidates for validation

- Optimizely Web Experimentation learning and certification materials.
- CXL CRO and experimentation courses (paid access; verify current terms).
- Experiment Nation exam (supplementary knowledge check, not a substitute for practical evidence).
- Google Analytics documentation and available practice environments.
- Microsoft Clarity as a practice/diagnostic tool, not a professional credential.
- Reputable statistics and experimentation references. Platform-specific thresholds or test-duration recommendations must not be generalized as universal rules.

### Traditional Media candidates for validation

- Thinkbox TV Masters (verify current cohort dates, access window, exam, and CPD conditions).
- RAB Audio Marketing Professional (verify eligibility, price, and assessment details).
- OAAA measurement and analytics guidance and current MRC standards (verify market applicability).
- Relevant local audience measurement providers and industry bodies. For Indonesia, verify methodology, access, coverage, currency, and limitations before making any resource mandatory.

### Credential classification

Classify every credential as one of: professional/proctored certification; course-completion certificate; skills badge/platform learning achievement; knowledge assessment; or award/competition recognition. These categories are not interchangeable.

## 7. Assessment architecture

The companion full-draft document now includes a common five-dimension learner rubric (Technical accuracy; Deliverable completeness; Analysis and judgement; Evidence, QA and governance; Communication and handoff), two knowledge checks with answer guidance per chapter, and chapter-specific applied artifacts. The proposed 80/100 pass mark and reviewer anchors are not calibrated until SME scoring and learner-pilot evidence are available.

Every chapter should include:
- A knowledge check for concepts and terminology.
- A practical artifact demonstrating the stated competency.
- A rubric with observable criteria and performance levels.
- A source/evidence requirement for factual claims.
- A reflection on limitations, assumptions, and trade-offs.

Capstones should assess problem framing, evidence quality, technical/operational feasibility, measurement validity, commercial reasoning, communication, and governance. A multiple-choice quiz alone is insufficient evidence of advanced competence.
