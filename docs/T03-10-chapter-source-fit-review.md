# T03 — Media Planning: 10-Chapter Source-Fit Review
**Review date:** 5 November 2026  
**Scope:** DPA-T03-CH01 through DPA-T03-CH10; current chapter objectives, practices and mapped resource sources.  
**Disposition:** FIRST-PASS DESK REVIEW COMPLETE; all ten chapter fits remain PARTIAL; formal acceptance remains OPEN.

## Executive finding

T03 has a relevant but incomplete source base. The current map includes The Trade Desk Programmatic 101, Google Ads Reach Planner, Nielsen Indonesia audience-measurement material and MRC standards for Out-of-Home and digital audio. The new source pass adds a 2025 Indonesia media-consumption report summary and the October 2026 Reach Planner suggestions update.

The principal issue is **currency compatibility**. A platform reach forecast, a TV peoplemeter panel, OOH audience estimates and digital-audio streaming impressions are not interchangeable or additive by default. Every estimate needs its own universe, population, geography, time window, measurement method, exclusions, deduplication and commercial scope.

No chapter is marked DIRECT FIT overall. A few sources directly support a narrow component (e.g., OOH standards for OOH metrics and Google Reach Planner for Google video forecast), but all ten chapters have material source gaps.

## Source evidence and limitations

### 1. Programmatic 101 — The Trade Desk Edge Academy

Official course page describes a one-hour introductory module covering the programmatic ecosystem, actors, data, identity, privacy and future direction. This is useful for digital/programmatic supply-chain literacy, not a complete cross-channel media-planning or publisher-buying curriculum.

Source: [Programmatic 101](https://edgeacademy.thetradedesk.com/programmatic-101).

### 2. Google Ads Reach Planner

Official Help explains Reach Planner is designed for YouTube and video-partner video campaign plans, with forecast metrics such as reach, frequency, views and conversions. It says forecasts are estimates, the tool is not available in every region or account, and data is updated weekly. The current suggestion page describes new frequency suggestion cards and an average-weekly-frequency feature (October 2026). Those product features are platform-specific; they are not a universal TV, radio, OOH or cross-media audience currency.

Sources: [About Reach Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9427120?hl=en) and [About suggestions in Reach Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9427192?hl=en).

### 3. Indonesia media-consumption context

YouGov's 13 August 2025 report page says the Indonesia study explores live/on-demand TV, radio, podcasts and social-platform habits, with demographic variation. The public summary was readable; full report download is routed through a separate platform sign-up/download link. This is consumer media-habit research, not a currency for purchased reach, ratings, GRP, CPP or unduplicated cross-media impressions.

Source: [YouGov — State of Indonesia Media Consumption 2025](https://yougov.com/reports/52747-id-media-consumption-report-2025).

### 4. MRC Out-of-Home Measurement Standards

The Media Rating Council's combined Phase 1 & 2 OOH standard is final, December 2025. Its contents cover OOH measurement definitions, impressions (gross, viewable/OTS and likelihood-to-see), audience and extended metrics including reach/frequency. It is a relevant international standards source for OOH measurement concepts; it does not prove Indonesia-specific adoption, local supplier accreditation, data availability or a common currency with Indonesian TV/radio/platform reports.

Source: [MRC OOH Measurement Standards — Phase 1 & 2 Combined, Final December 2025 (PDF)](https://mediaratingcouncil.org/sites/default/files/Standards/MRC%20OOH%20Standards%20Combined_FINAL.pdf).

### 5. MRC Digital Audio Measurement Standards

The MRC/IAB/RAB/NAB Digital Audio Measurement Standards v1.0 are dated January 2018. The standard covers browser/app streaming and progressive-download digital audio, measurement assets, audience, sessions, invalid traffic and cross-platform considerations. Crucially, it explicitly states it does **not** apply to digital AM/FM broadcasts delivered over-the-air in a traditional manner, or satellite radio. It therefore helps distinguish digital-streaming audio from traditional broadcast radio; it does not close the local AM/FM audience-rating gap.

Source: [MRC Digital Audio Measurement Standards v1.0 (PDF)](https://mediaratingcouncil.org/sites/default/files/Standards/Digital%20Audio%20Measurement%20Standards%20-%20Final%20Version%201.0.pdf).

### 6. Indonesia TV and radio measurement caveats

Nielsen's own 2023 Indonesia TV measurement release described a Peoplemeter panel of more than 12,000 homes and coverage representing an estimated audience; this is a provider-published historical description and must not be treated as a current 2026 panel specification without revalidation. Nielsen's Indonesia streaming-content-ratings release is from 2022 and is likewise dated.

A BPS SIRUSA statistical-activity metadata result indexed in this review refers to Nielsen TV audience measurement, a longitudinal panel and a 2025 collection / April 2026 dissemination schedule. The underlying record could not be directly opened in this check, so it is not counted as learner-verified access. It also refers to a specific TV-related activity rather than a complete industry cross-media currency.

The KPI's 15 August 2025 article describes development of a Public Interest, Needs and Comfort (MKK) survey instrument and notes that radio needed a distinct instrument from television. That survey is for public-service/broadcasting evaluation; it is **not** a commercial radio advertising ratings currency and must not be substituted for one.

Sources: [Nielsen — TV audience measurement in Indonesia (2023)](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2023/nielsen-more-than-doubles-tv-audience-measurement-in-indonesia/), [Nielsen — Streaming Content Ratings Indonesia (2022)](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2022/nielsen-launches-streaming-content-ratings-enabling-cross-media-measurement-in-indonesia/), [BPS SIRUSA statistical metadata](https://sirusa.web.bps.go.id/metadata/kegiatan/dc81f9bc-40c5-d068-4652-85607dbb6d98), [KPI — MKK public survey development for radio (15 August 2025)](https://kpi.go.id/news/kpi-siapkan-pengukuran-survey-mkk-untuk-radio).

## Required remediation

1. Obtain current Indonesia TV, radio (broadcast and streaming separately), OOH and publisher audience-methodology documents from the relevant measurement providers/media owners. Record sample, universe, geography, fieldwork dates, weighting, rating definition, reach/frequency rules and audit/accreditation status.
2. Establish a cross-media comparison protocol: reporting period, target universe, overlap/duplication method, co-viewing/device logic, exposure/viewability definition, ad-supported inventory and confidence intervals. Never sum platform reach as unique people without a documented deduplication model.
3. Add local media-owner source evidence for inventory, availability, rate basis, booking lead times, commercial terms and reporting. Distinguish direct publisher buys, broadcaster schedules, OOH inventory and DSP deals.
4. Build a reproducible synthetic case with separate data tables for TV, radio, streaming audio, OOH, online video, publisher display and search. Include a declared denominator per source and a deliberately unresolved cross-media overlap to teach the limits of unsupported aggregation.
5. Require qualified media-measurement SME review and learner access checks from Indonesia before any T03 resource or chapter acceptance.
