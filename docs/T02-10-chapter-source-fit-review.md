## Executive finding

T02's existing source set is mostly broad learning routes: Meta Blueprint's Media Planner catalogue, LinkedIn Marketing Academy, UK Government GovS 011 and ICC/ESOMAR 2025. The new source enrichment adds the GCS OASIS planning guide, the GCS Evaluation Cycle (February 2024), the ICC Advertising and Marketing Communications Code (2024), NN/g Journey Mapping 101, AMA brand-positioning reference, AAMA Positioning & Messaging Framework and the official 2025 ICC/ESOMAR Code PDF route.

The sources now better support specific components of campaign planning, audience research ethics, brand positioning, journey mapping and communication governance. They do **not** constitute a complete commercial connection-planning syllabus. Their contexts differ: GCS/GovS are for UK government communications; NN/g is UX-oriented journey mapping; AMA/AAMA are professional-association resources; ICC Codes establish ethical/self-regulatory boundaries rather than prescribing a complete campaign workflow. These references must not be presented as Indonesian legal standards or as complete agency operating procedures.

All ten chapters remain PARTIAL at desk-review level. Formal objective-to-source fit, access from Indonesia, local commercial/market relevance and qualified SME sign-off remain OPEN.

## Source evidence and limitations

### 1. GovS 011 — Communications functional standard (UK Government)

Version 2.1 was updated 30 November 2023. It applies to UK government departments and arm's-length bodies, and covers communication planning and management. Its campaign lifecycle includes objectives, audience insight, proposition, messages, channel strategy, implementation constraints, accountabilities and measurement; it recommends a channel-agnostic approach before channel selection.

Use: campaign lifecycle, governance, audience-first planning and accountability.

Limit: its defined scope is government communication. It is not a commercial brand strategy, FMCG agency brief, media investment model or Indonesian law.

Source: [GovS 011 — Communications](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-functional-standard-govs-011-communication/government-functional-standard-govs-011-communications).

### 2. Government Communication Service — OASIS campaign-planning guide (2020)

The OASIS guide structures planning around Objectives, Audience/Insight, Strategy/Idea, Implementation and Scoring/Evaluation. It discusses SMART objectives, audience segmentation, insight, proposition/messaging, channels, audience journeys, implementation and evaluation.

Use: a practical planning sequence and checklist for communication campaigns.

Limit: public-sector oriented and dated 2020; not a neutral standard for all commercial agency or brand-management practice.

Source: [GCS Guide to Campaign Planning — OASIS (PDF)](https://gcs.civilservice.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Guide-to-Campaign-Planning-OASIS-Framework.pdf).

### 3. GCS Evaluation Cycle (February 2024)

The document links evaluation to OASIS and recommends that evaluation design begin when objectives and audiences are set, not only after campaign delivery. It covers evaluation processes and metrics for government communication activities.

Use: measurement planning, learning loop and evaluation timing.

Limit: government communications context; not a substitute for brand-lift survey methods, causal experiments, incrementality, attribution, MMM or commercial ROI validation.

Source: [GCS Evaluation Cycle (PDF)](https://www.communications.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2024-02-13-GCS-Evaluation-Cycle-FINAL-OFFICIAL.pdf).

### 4. ICC/ESOMAR International Code on Market, Opinion and Social Research and Data Analytics (2025)

The 2025 Code describes global ethical/professional principles for research and data analytics, including fairness, transparency, privacy, participant rights, accuracy and professional responsibility. The official ESOMAR page showed a 2 November 2026 update when searched; the ESOMAR page and linked official PDF did not return a direct readable response in this check. The existing ICC/ESOMAR 2025 source route remains mapped, but direct Indonesia learner access to the PDF is not yet confirmed.

Use: research ethics, participant/data protection and integrity of evidence collection.

Limit: ethical code, not a research methods manual. It does not teach sampling, interviewing, survey design, segmentation analytics or insight synthesis by itself.

Sources: [ESOMAR Code page](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct) and [official 2025 PDF route](https://standards.esomar.org/assets/documents/icc-esomar-code-2025.pdf).

### 5. ICC Advertising and Marketing Communications Code (2024)

The ICC says the 2024 update covers responsible marketing across advertising, digital communications, sponsorship, sales promotion and social media, including areas such as influencer marketing, AI, environmental claims and marketing to children/teenagers.

Use: claims responsibility, ethical creative/marketing communication and broad cross-channel conduct.

Limit: global self-regulatory code; it does not replace Indonesian law, platform policy, product-specific legal substantiation or a company's approval process.

Source: [ICC Advertising and Marketing Communications Code](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/the-icc-advertising-and-marketing-communications-code/).

### 6. Nielsen Norman Group — Journey Mapping 101

The article describes a journey map as a visualization of the process a person follows to reach a goal, built from actions, thoughts and emotions into a narrative. Its search-indexed page shows a last review date of 15 July 2026; direct page retrieval returned a cache miss in this check.

Use: basic journey-map concepts and UX/user-experience framing.

Limit: it is UX-oriented and does not itself provide cross-channel media reach, paid/owned/earned touchpoint planning, media duplication, attribution or commercial-effectiveness measurement.

Source: [NN/g Journey Mapping 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-101/).

### 7. AMA and AAMA — Brand positioning and messaging

The AMA public page defines brand positioning as the market position relative to competitors and connects it to communication of value and benefits to a target audience. The page also distinguishes free general content from members-only resources.

The AAMA Positioning & Messaging Framework provides a detailed practice sequence: audience, need, insight, competitive frame, offer, primary/supporting benefits, point of difference, reasons to believe, positioning statement, core/supporting messages, proof points, objections and message hierarchy. Its direct page retrieval returned a cache miss in this check, so treat it as a supporting association resource, not a verified standard.

Use: structured prompts for positioning and messaging development.

Limit: professional-association guidance is not an independently validated universal method. AMA summary content is accessible, while deeper materials may require membership/payment; AAMA page access needs a learner-side check.

Sources: [AMA — Brand and Branding](https://www.ama.org/topics/brand-and-branding/) and [AAMA — Positioning & Messaging Framework](https://americanadvertisingandmarketingassociation.org/resources/planning-strategy/positioning-messaging-framework/).
